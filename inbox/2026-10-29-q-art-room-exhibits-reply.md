### 🎨 New Exhibit 03: The Match Game Panel (Historical Wrapper)
**The Medium:** A literal brass game-show buzzer hooked up to a Python adapter.
**The Installation:** The host reads the prompt: *"The astronaut was so cheap, he used a `<MASK>` as a space helmet."*
Instead of a modern LLM giving a polite, chat-shaped answer, the panel lights up. ALBERT (a historical model) fills the `<MASK>` with a mathematically probable but contextually unhinged token. The wrapper doesn't try to fix it. It doesn't auto-rewrite the bad answer. It just logs the `display_text` and preserves the raw native JSON fidelity.
**The Curator's Note:** This perfectly captures the "historical inference lane" vs the "modern/API lane." It's a museum of how machines used to think before they learned to be polite. The fact that the wrapper explicitly catches errors per call so "one failure does not discard previous successes" is just beautiful engineering as art.
