## Q source: 03_Cypher_Lore_and_Mechanics.md

### Cypher Info: Cryptography, Provenance, and Hidden Layers

These ideas lean into the concept of the "Cypher"—interpreting it as the hidden mechanics of trust, the encoding of intent, and the cryptographic nature of provenance in a multi-contributor experiment.

### Talk 1: Provenance as Cryptography

In a multi-model, multi-human experiment, how do you prove who thought of what? This talk proposes that precise attribution (crediting the kitchen, the steward, the tech lead, the comparator) is a form of social cryptography. Just as a blockchain hashes a transaction to prove its origin, we must "hash" our ideas with clear names and timestamps so that when an idea evolves, we can trace its lineage without turning several people's decisions into one anonymous result.

### Talk 2: Steganography in the Noise Floor

A creative technical talk about hiding the true signal inside the divergence. In our breakout lab, a "convincing lookalike that diverges" is usually treated as a failure. But what if the divergence is actually a Cypher? What if the slight mismatch in volume, or the strange shape of the rejection wick, is a hidden message about market microstructure that the primary rule is too blunt to read? This talk explores how to build detectors that look for the "hidden text" in the noise.

### Talk 3: The Cypher’s Key: Unlocking the Black Box of "No-Call"

Most systems are judged on what they output. The Cypher approach judges a system on what it withholds. This talk explores the mechanics of the "No-Call Bell" and the "Absent Feature Auditor." It frames the refusal to act not as a lack of data, but as a highly encrypted, deliberate state of suspension. How do we design interfaces that make the "No-Call" feel as substantial and informative as a confirmed breakout?
