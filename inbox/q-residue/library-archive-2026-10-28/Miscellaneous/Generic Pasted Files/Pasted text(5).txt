🛡️ The reductionist core: Regicide
Harold Godwinson was the crowned king of England. William kills him in battle.
That’s regicide. Full stop.
Even the Bayeux Tapestry literally labels the moment: Harold rex interfectus est — “King Harold is killed.”

William needed legitimacy, not “I killed the king and took his stuff.”

The Church needed a just war, not a dynastic mugging.

Norman aristocrats needed a legal fiction to justify land seizures.

So the narrative becomes: “Harold was a usurper; William was restoring rightful order.”  
That’s the medieval equivalent of a corporate hostile takeover being rebranded as “strategic restructuring.”

⚔️ The Cromwell Quote: What It Really Means
“We will cut off his head with the crown upon it.”  
(Attributed to Cromwell during the trial of Charles I.)

This is Cromwell doing three things at once:

1. Abolishing the idea of sacred monarchy
He’s saying:
“The crown doesn’t protect you. The crown is why we’re killing you.”

That’s a direct attack on the doctrine of divine right — the idea that kings are untouchable because God said so.

This is Cromwell’s whole vibe:
Kingship is just a job, not a divine species.

2. Weaponizing symbolism
Cutting off the king’s head with the crown still on is basically:

“We’re not killing Charles Stuart the man.”

“We’re killing the institution of monarchy.”

It’s political theater — the 1649 version of “delete the whole folder.”

3. A flex to Parliament and the Army
Cromwell is telling the moderates:
“We’re not negotiating anymore.”

The quote is a warning shot at the Presbyterians who wanted a settlement with the king.

He’s saying:
“If you won’t end the monarchy, we will.”

That’s why the line hits so hard — it’s not bravado, it’s policy.

🩸 Why It’s Darkly Funny
Because Cromwell is basically saying:

“We’re doing regicide, but we’re doing it properly.”

It’s the bureaucratic confidence that makes it hilarious.
It’s like someone saying:

“We’re not just firing him. We’re deleting his employee ID while he’s still at his desk.”

📜 The Backstory in One Breath
Charles I believed kings were sacred.

Cromwell believed kings were problems.

So when Charles refused to accept limits on his power, Cromwell’s faction decided:

“Fine. We’ll remove the power by removing the head.”
