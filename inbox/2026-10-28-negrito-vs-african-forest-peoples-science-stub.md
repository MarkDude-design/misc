# Science stub — African forest peoples (Baka) vs Philippine Negritos (Aeta)

Origin: 2023 documentary on the African forest peoples (Baka, Aka, Mbuti, Twa); Bantu neighbors call them "the little genius of the forest." Sparked by the folk assumption that they're related to the original inhabitants of the Philippines (Aeta/Ati) — and the counter-logic that they aren't, with the genuine surprise being how genetically FAR the Aeta are from African populations.

## Core finding (counter to the folk assumption)
Convergence, not kinship. Dark skin + short stature evolved independently twice (rainforest Africa vs tropical-foraging Asia). "Negrito" was a colonial surface-read label, now dropped as a bundle. Negritos are East-Eurasian/Australasian in ancestry — closer to Papuans and other Asians than to any African population.

## The genuinely novel fact
Ayta Magbukon carry the highest Denisovan ancestry of any living population — ~30–40% more than Papuans/Australians, pointing to a separate archaic-admixture event in Island SE Asia, later diluted by East Asian admixture ~2,300 years ago.

## African side
Deep sub-Saharan lineages: mtDNA L1c near-100% in several Baka/Aka groups; ancient Shum Laka foragers hint Central African hunter-gatherers diverged possibly ~250kya.

## Caveat on language-as-control
Mirror-image trap in both regions: both groups language-shifted to farmer languages (Ubangian/Bantu in Africa; Austronesian in the Philippines). Language comparison measures contact with expanding farmers, not deep ancestry. Parallel structure worth keeping: foragers hemmed in by agriculturalists on two continents. Possible pre-shift substrate: ~30% of Aka vocab isn't Bantu (forest/botanical/honey terms shared with Baka).

## Research angles if picked up
- Denisovan-admixture genetics as the "original inhabitants of the Philippines" lens
- Pre-Austronesian substrate: vocab, oral tradition, material culture
- Parallel social histories: foragers surrounded by expanding farmers on two continents
- Mark's planned control: language comparison "and that sort of stuff" for finding things novelty-connected to the first Filipinos
